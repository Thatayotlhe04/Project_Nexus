Overview

The Black-Scholes model is a mathematical framework for pricing European-style options. Developed by Fisher Black, Myron Scholes, and Robert Merton in the early 1970s, it calculates the theoretical value of options based on the underlying asset's spot price, strike price, time to expiration, risk-free interest rate, and volatility.

  • The underlying asset follows a geometric Brownian motion with constant drift and volatility.
  • No dividends are paid during the life of the option.
  • Markets are frictionless (no transaction costs or taxes).
  • Trading is continuous with no restrictions.
  • No arbitrage opportunities exist.
  • The risk-free interest rate is constant and known.
  • Options are European-style (exercised only at expiration).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Black-Scholes model?

The Black-Scholes model is a mathematical framework for pricing European-style options. It calculates theoretical option values based on spot price, strike price, risk-free rate, volatility, and time to expiration.

Why are my results different from other calculators?

Differences may arise from rounding, different calculation methods, or input precision. This calculator uses the Abramowitz-Stegun approximation for the normal distribution, accurate to ~7 decimal places.

What do the Greek letters mean?

S = Spot Price (current asset price), K = Strike Price, r = Risk-free rate, σ (sigma) = Volatility, T = Time to maturity in years.

Can I use this for American options?

No, this calculator is specifically for European-style options. American options can be exercised at any time before expiration and require different pricing models.

What volatility should I use?

Historical volatility is calculated from past price movements. Implied volatility is derived from market option prices. Both can affect the model's accuracy.
